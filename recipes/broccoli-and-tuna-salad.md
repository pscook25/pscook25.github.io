# Broccoli and Tuna Salad

Crisp-tender broccoli, flaked tuna, chickpeas and toasted almonds in a creamy, tangy Greek yoghurt and lemon dressing. A crunchy, make-ahead lunch with nearly 60 g protein and 17 g fibre.

**Source:** Recipe card, "Broccoli and Tuna Salad" (author unknown; serves 2)

*Plan for at least 1 hour in the fridge before serving.*

## Ingredients

### Salad
- **900 g (about 3 heads)** broccoli, cut into small florets
- **3 × 185 g cans** tuna in springwater, drained (about 400 g drained)
- **400 g can** no-added-salt chickpeas, drained and rinsed
- **100 g (1 small)** red onion, finely chopped
- **100 g** almonds, chopped
- **2** spring onions, thinly sliced

### Dressing
- **500 g** fat-free plain Greek yoghurt (e.g. Chobani 0%)
- **40 mL (2 tbsp)** red wine vinegar
- **1 lemon**, zested and juiced (about 20 mL juice)
- **1 g (pinch)** salt
- **1 tsp** cracked black pepper

## Method
1. Bring a large saucepan of water to the boil.
2. Add the broccoli and blanch for 1–2 min, until bright green and just tender-crisp.
3. Drain, then plunge into a bowl of iced water for 2 min. Drain well and pat dry.
4. Meanwhile, toast the almonds in a dry frying pan over medium heat for 3–4 min, stirring, until golden. Cool.
5. In a large bowl, combine the broccoli, tuna, chickpeas, red onion and almonds.
6. In a small bowl, mix the yoghurt, vinegar, lemon zest, lemon juice, salt and pepper.
7. Pour the dressing over the broccoli mixture and stir until evenly coated.
8. Cover and refrigerate for about 1 hour, until chilled.
9. Scatter with spring onion just before serving.

## Storage & Meal Prep
- **Portion:** about 600 g
- **Fridge:** 3 days in airtight containers · **Freezer:** not suitable
- **Reheat:** not needed, serve cold
- **Cook ahead vs fresh:** make the whole salad ahead (it improves after a few hours). For the crunchiest almonds, keep them separate and stir through at serving. Add the spring onion fresh.

## Changes from original
- **Scaled from 2 serves to 4**, converted to Australian metric. Green onion is called spring onion here.
- **Broccoli reduced from 3 US cups per serve to 225 g per serve, and blanched**: the card didn't say raw or cooked. A quick blanch keeps it crunchy but easier to eat.
- **Almonds reduced from ¼ US cup per serve (35 g) to 25 g per serve, and toasted**: trims fat and energy to make room for chickpeas.
- **Tuna set at 100 g drained per serve**, specified as tuna in springwater and as Australian 185 g cans.
- **Added no-added-salt chickpeas (100 g per serve)**: lifts energy into the 500–650 kcal range and adds fibre.
- **Greek yoghurt specified as fat-free**: keeps sat fat at 2 g.
- **Lemon pepper (1 tsp) replaced with lemon zest, lemon juice and black pepper, and salt cut to a pinch**: most lemon pepper seasonings are high in salt.
- **"Red vinegar" read as red wine vinegar.**

## Notes & swaps
- **No-added-salt tuna:** use John West or Sirena no-added-salt tuna to cut sodium by about 250 mg per serve. No change to protein.
- **Vegetarian:** replace the tuna with a second can of chickpeas and 200 g crumbled firm tofu. Protein drops to roughly 33 g per serve.
- **Dairy-free:** use a plain high-protein soy yoghurt. Protein drops about 5 g per serve.
- **Nut-free:** replace the almonds with 100 g pepitas (pumpkin seeds). Protein rises about 2 g per serve.
- **Budget:** use frozen broccoli florets (blanch 1 min from frozen) and a 425 g can of tuna instead of 3 small cans.
