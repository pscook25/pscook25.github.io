# Beef Lasagna with Fresh Pasta and Bechamel

Layers of tender homemade wholemeal-and-plain pasta sheets rolled on your stand mixer, a rich beef, mushroom and red lentil ragu, and a light skim-milk bechamel under a golden mozzarella crust. The lentils, spinach and wholemeal flour add the fibre, and the eggs in the pasta lift the protein, while an olive oil roux and reduced-fat cheese keep saturated fat under control.

**Source:** Adapted from "Beef Lasagna with Homemade Bechamel" (original source not stated)

## Ingredients

### Fresh pasta sheets
- **100 g (about ⅔ cup)** wholemeal plain flour
- **100 g (about ⅔ cup)** plain flour
- **2** large eggs (700 g carton size)
- **Up to 20 mL (1 tbsp)** cold water, only if the dough is dry
- **20 g (2 tbsp)** plain flour, extra for dusting (most is brushed off, not counted in nutrition)

### Meat sauce
- **5 mL (1 tsp)** olive oil
- **1 (150 g)** brown onion, finely diced
- **1 (120 g)** carrot, finely grated
- **3 cloves** garlic, crushed
- **250 g** white cup mushrooms, finely chopped
- **300 g** 5-star lean beef mince (5% fat or less)
- **40 g (2 tbsp)** salt-reduced tomato paste
- **400 g** tin crushed tomatoes, no added salt
- **250 mL (1 cup)** passata, no added salt
- **50 g (¼ cup)** dried red lentils, rinsed
- **125 mL (½ cup)** salt-reduced beef stock
- **125 mL (½ cup)** water
- **2 tsp** dried Italian herbs (or 1 tsp oregano + 1 tsp basil)
- **1** bay leaf (optional)
- Black pepper, to taste

### Bechamel
- **10 mL (2 tsp)** olive oil
- **30 g (¼ cup)** plain flour
- **600 mL** skim milk, warmed
- **20 g** parmesan, finely grated
- **Pinch** ground nutmeg
- Ground white pepper (or black), to taste

### Assembly
- **120 g** baby spinach (one bag)
- **60 g** reduced-fat mozzarella, grated
- Olive oil spray, for greasing the dish

## Method

### Fresh pasta dough (stand mixer)
1. Fit the stand mixer with the flat beater (paddle).
2. Add the wholemeal flour and 100 g plain flour to the bowl.
3. Crack in the eggs.
4. Mix on speed 1–2 for 1–2 minutes until the mixture looks like coarse, shaggy crumbs.
5. If the crumbs don't clump when squeezed, add the water 1 tsp at a time, mixing briefly between additions.
6. Swap the paddle for the dough hook.
7. Knead on speed 2 for 5–6 minutes until the dough is smooth, firm and springs back when poked.
8. Shape the dough into a flat disc and wrap it tightly in plastic wrap.
9. Rest at room temperature for at least 30 minutes (up to 2 hours). Start the sauce while it rests.

### Meat sauce
10. Heat the oil in a large, deep frying pan over medium heat.
11. Add the onion and carrot and cook for 5 minutes until soft.
12. Add the garlic and mushrooms and cook for 5 minutes until the mushrooms release their liquid and it evaporates.
13. Turn the heat to high, add the mince and brown for 5–6 minutes, breaking it up as you go, until no pink remains.
14. Stir in the tomato paste and cook for 1 minute.
15. Add the crushed tomatoes, passata, red lentils, stock, water, herbs and bay leaf.
16. Simmer uncovered on low for 30 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the lentils have broken down and the sauce is thick but still spoonable (not dry, as the fresh pasta absorbs moisture). Add a splash of water if it catches on the base.
17. Season with black pepper, then remove the bay leaf.

### Roll the pasta sheets (while the sauce simmers)
18. Cut the rested dough into 4 equal pieces. Keep 3 wrapped while you work with 1.
19. Flatten the piece into a rectangle about 1 cm thick and dust lightly with the extra flour.
20. Fit the pasta roller attachment to the mixer's power hub and set the roller to the widest setting (1).
21. Run the mixer on speed 2 and feed the dough through the roller.
22. Fold the sheet in thirds and feed it through setting 1 again. Repeat 2–3 times until the sheet is smooth.
23. Roll the sheet once through each setting from 2 up to 5, dusting lightly with flour if it sticks. The sheet should be thin and slightly translucent when held to the light.
24. Cut the sheet into 20 cm lengths.
25. Lay the lengths on a floured tray and cover with a clean tea towel.
26. Repeat with the remaining 3 pieces. You will have about 6–8 sheets, roughly 14 cm wide.

### Bechamel
27. Heat the 10 mL olive oil in a medium saucepan over medium heat.
28. Whisk in the 30 g flour and cook, stirring, for 1 minute without browning.
29. Add the warm milk about 100 mL at a time, whisking constantly to avoid lumps.
30. Keep whisking over medium heat for 5–7 minutes until the sauce coats the back of a spoon. It will be pourable.
31. Take off the heat and stir in the parmesan and nutmeg. Season with white pepper.

### Assemble and bake
32. Preheat the oven to 200°C conventional (180°C fan-forced).
33. Lightly spray a 20 x 30 cm baking dish with olive oil.
34. Spread about ½ cup (125 mL) of the meat sauce thinly over the base.
35. Lay a layer of pasta sheets over the sauce, side by side, trimming to fit and patching gaps with offcuts.
36. Top with half the remaining meat sauce, half the spinach and one-third of the bechamel.
37. Add a second layer of pasta sheets.
38. Top with the rest of the meat sauce, the rest of the spinach and another third of the bechamel.
39. Add a final layer of pasta sheets and spread over the remaining bechamel.
40. Scatter the mozzarella over the top.
41. Cover with foil and bake for 35 minutes.
42. Remove the foil and bake another 15 minutes until golden and bubbling, and the pasta is tender when a knife slides through the centre.
43. Check the centre reaches at least 75°C.
44. Rest for 15 minutes before cutting so the layers hold.

## Storage & Meal Prep
- **Portion:** approx. 620 g per serve (a quarter of the dish)
- **Fridge:** 4 days in airtight containers · **Freezer:** 3 months (freeze single portions once fully cooled)
- **Reheat:** Chilled portion, covered, in the microwave on high for 3–4 minutes, rest 1 minute, then check the centre reaches 75°C. Or bake covered with foil at 180°C (160°C fan-forced) for 20–25 minutes. Thaw frozen portions overnight in the fridge first.
- **Cook ahead vs fresh:** The dough keeps wrapped in the fridge for 2 days (bring to room temperature 30 minutes before rolling). The ragu and bechamel can be made 2 days ahead (press cling wrap onto the bechamel surface). Roll the sheets the same day, or layer them between baking paper and chill for up to 24 hours. Assemble up to a day ahead, cover and chill, then add 10 minutes to the covered bake time. Add the mozzarella just before baking.

## Changes from original
- **Pasta:** Replaced 200 g instant (no-boil) sheets with fresh sheets from 100 g wholemeal and 100 g plain flour plus 2 eggs, made in the stand mixer and rolled with the pasta roller. Wholemeal adds fibre and the eggs add protein. The sheets go in raw, with a looser sauce to cook them.
- **Scaled from 6 to 4 serves** (mince 500 g to 300 g, with the rest of the quantities adjusted to suit).
- **Mince:** Now 5-star lean (5% fat or less) to cut saturated fat.
- **Added 50 g dried red lentils** to the ragu for fibre and protein, along with an extra 125 mL water for them to absorb.
- **Bechamel:** Butter (50 g) became 10 mL olive oil, full-cream milk (750 mL) became 600 mL skim, flour dropped from 50 g to 30 g and parmesan from 40 g to 20 g. This cuts saturated fat and calories. Skim milk thins the sauce slightly.
- **Mozzarella:** 150 g became 60 g reduced-fat, for saturated fat.
- **Passata:** 500 g became 250 mL to suit 4 serves and the lentils. Choose no-added-salt tomatoes and salt-reduced paste to keep sodium low.
- **Olive oil in the sauce:** 1 tbsp became 1 tsp.
- **Spinach:** 60 g became the full 120 g bag, for fibre.
- **Salt:** Removed from the recipe because the stock, cheese and tinned tomatoes already supply it.
- **Oven:** Added the conventional temperature (200°C) alongside fan-forced (180°C).
- **Bake time:** Covered time increased from 30 to 35 minutes so the fresh sheets cook through.
- **Layering:** Set at 3 pasta layers to match the amount of fresh dough, with a thin sauce layer on the base.
- Replaced the original "Macros per Serve" with a full nutrition table.

## Notes & swaps
- **No pasta roller attachment:** Roll the dough by hand with a rolling pin to about 1.5 mm and cut to size, or use 200 g instant lasagne sheets. Instant sheets lower protein by about 3 g, fibre by about 2 g and sat fat by about 1 g per serve.
- **Vegetarian:** Replace the mince with 2 x 400 g tins brown lentils, drained and rinsed, and use vegetable stock. Protein drops by about 5 g to roughly 39 g per serve, fibre rises by about 5 g, and energy rises by about 25 kcal.
- **Dairy-free:** Use unsweetened soy milk in the bechamel (similar protein) and omit the cheese or use a dairy-free melting cheese. Leaving out the cheese reduces protein by about 6 g per serve.
- **Budget:** Use all plain flour (fibre drops by about 2 g per serve, still above target) and a home-brand mince, passata and tomatoes. Check the mince label to stay at 5% fat or less.
- **Salt:** If you want to season with salt, ¼ tsp across the whole dish adds about 150 mg sodium per serve, which still sits under 700 mg.
- **Cheese:** Doubling the mozzarella to 120 g would push saturated fat above 6 g per serve.
