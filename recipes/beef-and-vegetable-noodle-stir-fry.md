# Beef & Vegetable Noodle Stir-Fry

Tender seared beef strips tossed with crisp snow peas, pak choy, capsicum and celery and thin egg noodles in a glossy ginger-soy sauce. Edamame and a generous beef-to-noodle ratio lift it to 44 g protein and 9 g fibre per serve.

**Source:** Original

## Ingredients
### Beef & marinade
- **500 g** beef stir-fry strips (rump or topside)
- **10 mL (2 tsp)** salt-reduced soy sauce
- **6 g (2 tsp)** cornflour

### Sauce
- **30 mL (1½ tbsp)** salt-reduced soy sauce
- **10 mL (2 tsp)** oyster sauce
- **10 mL (2 tsp)** rice vinegar
- **60 mL (¼ cup)** water
- **3 g (1 tsp)** cornflour
- **5 mL (1 tsp)** sesame oil

### Vegetables
- **250 g** snow peas, trimmed
- **3 baby pak choy (approx. 400 g)**, stems sliced, leaves left whole
- **40 g (1 handful)** baby spinach
- **2 green capsicums (approx. 300 g)**, cut into thin strips
- **½ red onion (approx. 60 g)**, thinly sliced
- **2 celery stalks (approx. 120 g)**, sliced diagonally
- **4 spring onions**, whites sliced, greens thinly sliced
- **3 garlic cloves**, finely chopped
- **20 g (thumb-sized piece)** fresh ginger, finely grated
- **150 g** frozen shelled edamame

### Noodles & cooking
- **250 g** dried thin egg noodles
- **20 mL (1 tbsp)** canola oil, divided

## Method
1. Combine the beef, 10 mL soy sauce and 6 g cornflour in a bowl. Stand for 10 min while you prep the vegetables.
2. Whisk the sauce ingredients (30 mL soy, oyster sauce, rice vinegar, water, 3 g cornflour and sesame oil) in a small jug.
3. Boil the egg noodles in a large pot of water for 3–4 min, or as the packet directs.
4. Add the frozen edamame to the noodle pot for the last 2 min of cooking.
5. Drain the noodles and edamame, rinse briefly under warm water and set aside.
6. Heat a large wok over high heat until very hot. Add 5 mL oil.
7. Sear half the beef in a single layer for 1–2 min, until browned. Transfer to a plate.
8. Repeat with 5 mL oil and the remaining beef, then transfer to the plate.
9. Add the remaining 10 mL oil to the wok. Stir-fry the red onion, celery and capsicum for 2 min, until starting to soften at the edges.
10. Add the garlic, ginger and spring onion whites. Stir-fry for 30 sec, until fragrant.
11. Add the pak choy stems and snow peas. Stir-fry for 2 min, until bright green and just tender-crisp.
12. Add the pak choy leaves and spinach. Toss for 30 sec, until just wilted.
13. Return the beef and any juices to the wok. Add the noodles, edamame and sauce.
14. Toss for 1–2 min, until the sauce thickens, coats everything and the beef is just cooked through (no pink in the centre).
15. Divide among 4 bowls and scatter with the spring onion greens.

## Storage & Meal Prep
- **Portion:** approx. 460 g per serve
- **Fridge:** 3 days in airtight containers · **Freezer:** not suitable (noodles and vegetables turn soft and watery)
- **Reheat:** microwave covered with 1 tbsp (20 mL) water for 2–3 min on high, stirring halfway, until steaming hot throughout (at least 75°C). Or toss in a hot wok for 3–4 min.
- **Cook ahead vs fresh:** cook the whole dish ahead and portion into containers once cooled. For the crispest result, keep the spring onion greens aside and add them after reheating.

## Changes from original
- Built around your fridge list: 500 g beef strips, snow peas, pak choy, spinach, green capsicum, red onion, spring onion and celery. Quantities for pak choy, capsicum, spinach and celery are my assumptions, so adjust to what you have.
- Scaled to 4 serves: 125 g raw beef per serve gives about 27 g protein from the beef alone.
- Chose thin egg noodles over rice noodles for more protein and fibre per serve.
- Added 250 g dried egg noodles (about 60 g dry per serve) to bring energy into the 500–650 kcal range.
- Added 150 g frozen edamame (Coles / Woolworths freezer aisle) to reach the fibre target of ≥8 g and add protein.
- Used salt-reduced soy sauce and only 10 mL oyster sauce to keep sodium under 700 mg per serve.
- Limited oil to 20 mL canola plus 5 mL sesame oil to keep saturated fat at about 3 g per serve.
- Velveted the beef lightly with cornflour and soy so it stays tender with a quick sear.

## Notes & swaps
- **Rice noodles:** use 250 g dried rice vermicelli or pad Thai noodles. Protein falls by about 4 g per serve and fibre by about 1 g, which leaves fibre right on the 8 g target.
- **Gluten-free:** use rice noodles, tamari instead of soy sauce, and check that your oyster sauce is gluten-free. Protein drops as for the rice noodle swap.
- **Vegetarian:** swap the beef for 600 g firm tofu, pressed and cubed, and the oyster sauce for vegetarian stir-fry sauce. Protein falls by about 10 g per serve, to roughly 33 g.
- **Budget:** swap the edamame for frozen peas. Protein falls by about 6 g per serve and fibre stays about the same. Chicken breast strips can replace the beef with a similar protein result.
- **Dairy-free:** already dairy-free.
