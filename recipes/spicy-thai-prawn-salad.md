# Spicy Thai Prawn Salad

Poached prawns, rice vermicelli, edamame and crunchy veg tossed in a hot-sour lime and fish sauce dressing with mint, coriander and chopped peanuts. Fresh and fiery, with 48 g protein and 12 g fibre.

**Source:** Recipe card, "Spicy Thai Shrimp Salad" (author unknown; serves 1)

| Energy | Protein | Carbs | Sugars | Fat | Sat fat | Fibre | Sodium |

*Estimates based on AUSNUT 2011–13 / FSANZ data, using raw prawns poached in unsalted water and unsalted peanuts.*

## Ingredients

### Prawns
- **600 g** raw peeled prawns, deveined, tails removed (about 1.2 kg whole raw prawns, or frozen raw peeled)

### Dressing
- **60 mL (3 tbsp)** lime juice (about 3 limes)
- **15 mL (3 tsp)** fish sauce
- **30 mL (1½ tbsp)** peanut oil
- **10 mL (2 tsp)** sesame oil
- **8 sachets** stevia (e.g. Natvia), or to taste
- **2 tsp** dried chilli flakes

### Salad
- **160 g** dried rice vermicelli noodles
- **300 g** frozen shelled edamame
- **240 g (2 × 120 g bags)** mixed salad leaves
- **300 g (2 Lebanese)** cucumbers, halved and sliced
- **240 g (2 medium)** carrots, julienned or coarsely grated
- **240 g (1 large)** red capsicum, thinly sliced
- **240 g (about ⅙ head)** red cabbage, finely shredded

### To serve
- **20 g (½ bunch)** mint, leaves picked
- **20 g (½ bunch)** coriander, leaves picked
- **40 g** unsalted roasted peanuts, roughly chopped

## Method
1. Bring a large saucepan of unsalted water to the boil.
2. Add the edamame and cook for 3 min. Lift out with a slotted spoon, rinse under cold water and drain.
3. Reduce the heat so the water is at a gentle simmer. Add the prawns and poach for 2–3 min, until pink, curled and opaque all the way through (internal temp 63°C).
4. Drain the prawns, then plunge into a bowl of iced water for 2 min to stop the cooking. Drain well and pat dry.
5. Put the rice vermicelli in a heatproof bowl, cover with boiling water and soak for 5–6 min until tender (or per packet).
6. Drain the noodles, rinse under cold water, drain well and snip into shorter lengths with kitchen scissors.
7. In a large bowl, whisk the lime juice, fish sauce, peanut oil, sesame oil, stevia and chilli flakes until the stevia dissolves.
8. Add the prawns, noodles, edamame, salad leaves, cucumber, carrot, capsicum and red cabbage to the dressing.
9. Add most of the mint and coriander, then toss gently to coat.
10. Divide among 4 bowls and scatter with the remaining herbs and the chopped peanuts.

## Storage & Meal Prep
- **Portion:** about 530 g
- **Fridge:** 2 days in airtight containers (dressing stored separately) · **Freezer:** not suitable
- **Reheat:** not needed, serve cold
- **Cook ahead vs fresh:** cook the prawns, edamame and noodles and keep them chilled. Make the dressing in a jar. Prep the cucumber, carrot, capsicum and cabbage ahead. Add the salad leaves, herbs and peanuts fresh, and dress just before eating so the leaves stay crisp.

## Changes from original
- **Scaled from 1 serve to 4**, converted to Australian metric. Shrimp is called prawns here.
- **Prawns reduced from 170 g to 150 g per serve, and specified as raw prawns poached in unsalted water**: protein is still 48 g. Pre-cooked supermarket prawns are usually cooked in brine and would push sodium over 700 mg.
- **Coconut oil (1 US tbsp per serve) replaced with peanut and sesame oil (10 mL per serve)**: coconut oil alone gave about 12 g sat fat per serve, and it sets solid on a cold salad. Sat fat is now about 3 g.
- **Fish sauce reduced from 20 mL to 15 mL in total**: keeps sodium at about 610 mg per serve.
- **"Veggie of choice" specified as cucumber, carrot, capsicum and red cabbage (240–300 g of each in total)**: the card gave no quantity.
- **Added edamame (75 g per serve)**: boosts protein and fibre.
- **Added rice vermicelli (40 g dry per serve) and unsalted peanuts (10 g per serve)**: the original was about 350 kcal and low in fibre. These bring it to 600 kcal and make it a full meal.
- **"Fresh herbs if desired" specified as mint and coriander** with quantities.
- **Crushed red pepper called dried chilli flakes**, the Australian name.

## Notes & swaps
- **Pre-cooked prawns:** use 480 g cooked peeled prawns and skip steps 3–4. Protein is similar, but sodium can rise by 200–400 mg per serve depending on brand. Rinse them and cut the fish sauce to 10 mL.
- **Sweetener:** replace the stevia with 2 tsp honey or brown sugar in total. Adds about 10 kcal and 3 g sugars per serve. No change to protein.
- **Coconut flavour:** use 10 mL melted coconut oil in place of the sesame oil. Sat fat rises to about 5 g per serve, still within target.
- **Vegetarian:** replace the prawns with 600 g firm tofu (pressed, cubed and pan-fried until golden) and use a vegan fish sauce or salt-reduced soy sauce. Protein drops to roughly 35 g per serve.
- **Budget:** use frozen raw peeled prawns (Aldi and Coles both stock them), or swap to 600 g chicken breast, poached and shredded. Protein stays about the same with less sodium.
- **Gluten-free:** as written, if your fish sauce is gluten-free (most are, but check the label).
- **Nut-free:** replace the peanuts with 1 tbsp toasted sesame seeds. Protein drops about 1 g per serve.
