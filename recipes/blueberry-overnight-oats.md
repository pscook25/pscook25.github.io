# Blueberry Overnight Oats

Layered jars of soft oats, juicy blueberries and thick vanilla Greek yoghurt set with chia, finished with crunchy toasted almonds. A grab-and-go breakfast with 37 g protein and 14 g fibre that's ready when you wake up.

**Source:** Recipe card, "Blueberry Overnight Oats" (author unknown; serves 1)

*Plan this the night before: the jars need at least 6 hours (overnight) in the fridge.*

## Ingredients

### Oat layer
- **200 g (2¼ cups)** rolled oats
- **400 mL** water

### Yoghurt layer
- **1 kg** fat-free plain Greek yoghurt (e.g. Chobani 0%)
- **60 g (⅓ cup)** chia seeds
- **20 mL (1 tbsp)** vanilla extract
- **8 sachets** stevia (e.g. Natvia), or to taste

### Fruit & topping
- **400 g** frozen blueberries
- **60 g** flaked or slivered almonds, toasted

## Method
1. Put the oats and water in a large microwave-safe bowl and stir.
2. Microwave on high for 2–3 min, stirring once halfway, until the oats are soft and the water is absorbed.
3. In a separate bowl, mix the Greek yoghurt, chia seeds, vanilla and stevia until smooth.
4. Divide the cooked oats among 4 jars or containers (about 150 g each) and press down lightly.
5. Spoon 100 g frozen blueberries over the oats in each jar.
6. Top each jar with a quarter of the yoghurt mixture (about 270 g) and smooth the surface.
7. Seal and refrigerate for at least 6 hours or overnight, until the chia has thickened the yoghurt and the berries have thawed.
8. Just before eating, scatter 15 g toasted almonds over each jar.

## Storage & Meal Prep
- **Portion:** about 535 g per jar
- **Fridge:** 4 days in sealed jars · **Freezer:** not suitable (the yoghurt separates)
- **Reheat:** not needed, eat cold. If you prefer it warm, transfer to a bowl and microwave for 1–1½ min, stirring halfway (the yoghurt may go slightly grainy).
- **Cook ahead vs fresh:** make all 4 jars on Sunday night for the working week. Keep the almonds in a separate container and add at serving so they stay crunchy.

## Changes from original
- **Scaled from 1 serve to 4**, converted to Australian metric.
- **Greek yoghurt reduced from 1¼ US cups (about 300 g) to 250 g per serve**: makes room for more oats while keeping protein at 37 g.
- **Oats increased from ¼ US cup (about 20 g) to 50 g per serve**, and the water quantity specified (the original gave none): adds fibre and energy.
- **Blueberries increased from ¼ US cup to 100 g per serve**, and specified as blueberries (the original said "berries").
- **Added chia seeds (15 g per serve) and toasted almonds (15 g per serve)**: the original was about 275 kcal with little fibre. These bring it to 570 kcal and 14 g fibre.
- **Removed "served with one side veggie"**: the card gave no vegetable or quantity, and the target fibre is now met without it.
- **Added a minimum fridge time** so the chia sets the yoghurt.

## Notes & swaps
- **Gluten-free:** use certified gluten-free oats. No change to protein.
- **Dairy-free:** use a high-protein soy yoghurt (e.g. Vitasoy or a soy Greek-style). Protein drops to roughly 25–28 g per serve, so add 15 g vanilla pea protein to each jar to stay above 30 g.
- **Nut-free:** replace the almonds with 15 g pepitas (pumpkin seeds) per serve. Protein rises about 1 g.
- **Budget:** use frozen mixed berries instead of blueberries. Fibre rises slightly, no change to protein.
- **Sweetener:** swap stevia for 1 tsp honey per serve. Adds about 30 kcal and 8 g sugars.
